Help & getting started

A quick guide to Readfine: how to set it up, how to get around the reader, and answers to the questions that come up most. For a complete rundown, see the full feature list.

{# ── Getting started ─────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

Getting started

  1. 1

    Add your first feed

    Paste an RSS/Atom feed URL in Settings → Feeds. Readfine can also scrape a website that has no RSS, see What's a scrape feed? below.

    Coming from another reader? Import your subscriptions via OPML.

  2. 2

    Read & organize

    Articles open in a clean reading view. Mark them read, star favourites, or archive what you want to keep out of the way.

    Labels are your own colour-coded tags for sorting articles by hand. Create them in Settings → Labels.

    Filters do the tagging for you: each new article is checked against your rules and, when the conditions match, actions are applied automatically (add a label, mark as read, star, archive). Set them up in Settings → Filters (see How do filters work?).

    That's everything you need for day-to-day reading.

  3. 3

    Turn on AI (optional)

    AI features are an optional layer. They use your own API key (Anthropic, OpenAI or Google), which you add in Settings → AI. See Where do I get an AI key? You can also run the models yourself, with no key at all, over your own endpoint.

    Once enabled you get:

    • Relevance scoring: each article gets a score based on your interests. A handy trick: add a filter that marks low-score articles as read, so they never clutter your list.
    • Summaries and chat over any article.
    • Catch me up & briefings: a digest of what you missed over a period, on demand or scheduled to your inbox.
{# ── Getting around the reader ───────────────────────────────────────── #}

Getting around the reader

Most of the reader is where you'd expect it. These few things are worth pointing out, because they only show up once you go looking.

Row actions in the sidebar
Hover over a feed, folder, label or one of the views at the top and two small buttons appear on the right: ↻ fetches that feed right away, ✓ marks everything as read. The ✓ always applies to the row you're on, so it works per feed, per folder, per label, or for all articles at once. On a touchscreen, press and hold the row instead of hovering.
Folding things away
The small arrow next to a folder or next to Labels collapses that group. The arrow in the sidebar header collapses the whole sidebar into a narrow strip of icons, which keeps your navigation one click away without taking up a panel.
The ··· menu on an article
Next to the article's date sits a ··· button. It holds everything beyond plain reading: star, archive, mark read or unread, share, copy the text, and, if the article came in as a bare headline, Extract full content to pull the full text from the original page. With an AI key it also offers the summary, context and chat.
Search from anywhere
Press / to open search without reaching for the mouse, or use the magnifier in the sidebar header. You can scope a search by feed, folder, label and read status, and sort the results. Esc closes it.
Keeping an article that isn't in your feeds
Open Saved in the sidebar and paste a link into the bar at the top of the list. See What is Saved?
{# ── Suggested workflow ──────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

A workflow that works well

There's no single right way to use Readfine, but here's one that scales nicely once you follow a lot of feeds:

  • Let filters (and, with AI, scoring) do the triage: auto-label what matters, mark the rest as read, then browse your labeled articles instead of wading through one long unread list.
  • Treat the star as a read-later queue: star anything worth a proper read, then come back to your starred list when you have time, rather than reading everything the moment it arrives.

FAQ

Can I import from another reader?

Yes. Export an OPML file from your current reader and import it in Settings → OPML. You can also export your Readfine subscriptions there.

What's a scrape feed?

When a website has no RSS feed, Readfine can read its listing page directly. You point it at the page and give a CSS selector that matches the article links; Readfine then checks that page on a schedule, just like an RSS feed. If you have an AI key, it can suggest the selector for you. Start by pasting the site URL in Settings → Feeds.

How do filters work?

A filter is a set of conditions (match on title, content, author, score… with AND/OR and optional regex) plus actions that run when an article matches: add a label, mark as read, or star. You can scope a filter to specific feeds or folders. Filters run on new articles as they arrive. Manage them in Settings → Filters.

A new filter only sees articles that arrive after you save it. To catch up on what's already in your list, use Apply next to the filter, which runs it over your existing articles once and shows you what it will do before anything is committed. Test next to it checks the conditions against your articles without changing a thing.

How do I use regex in a filter?

When a condition's operator is regex, the value is treated as a regular expression (Python syntax). The pattern is matched anywhere in the field and is case-insensitive, so einstein also matches “Einstein”. Use it on text fields (title, content, author, URL) when plain contains isn't enough.

  • (apple|pear|fig) matches any of several terms (OR).
  • \bAI\b matches the whole word “AI” only (not “rain” or “maintain”); \b is a word boundary.
  • ^Breaking means the field starts with “Breaking” (^ = start, $ = end).
  • colou?r matches “color” and “colour” (? = the previous item zero or one time).
  • \d{4} matches exactly four digits, e.g. a year like “2026” (\d = a digit, {4} = four times).

Building blocks you can combine: . any character, \d digit, \w letter/number, \s space, * zero or more, + one or more, [a-z] a range. Special characters (. $ ^ ( ) [ ] * + ?) must be escaped with a backslash to match them literally, e.g. \$\d+ matches a price like “$50”.

Patterns are limited to 200 characters, and a few constructs prone to catastrophic backtracking (nested quantifiers like (\w+)+) are rejected when you save. An invalid pattern is reported on save, so nothing breaks silently.

New to regex? Build and test patterns interactively at regex101.com. Pick the Python flavor to match how Readfine evaluates them.

How do I customize the layout?

Open Settings → Preferences to choose a 2- or 3-panel layout (separately for medium and large screens), the list density (compact, comfortable or summary, separately for desktop and mobile), dark mode, and whether articles are marked read as you scroll. You can also choose how the sidebar behaves on small screens (collapsible or hideable), and where the breakpoints between screen sizes sit.

The same page holds the smaller things that shape day-to-day reading: the font family and text size of the reading view, your timezone and how dates and numbers are written, the default sort order and how many articles load at a time, whether the list starts filtered to unread, whether Readfine jumps to the next feed after you mark everything read, and whether an article that carries only a headline opens the original page straight away.

What is Saved?

Saved is for articles that aren't in any of your feeds. Open it in the sidebar, paste a link into the bar at the top of the list, and Readfine fetches the page and runs it through the same readable extraction as a normal article. Saved articles stay until you remove them, so retention never takes them away.

Can I share an article?

Yes. Share in the article's ··· menu creates a link that works without signing in, so you can send it to someone who has no Readfine account. Anyone holding the link can read that one article, so treat it as public. The same menu shows the link once it exists, along with Revoke, which kills it immediately. Nothing else about your account is exposed, and no article is shared until you ask for it.

A feed shows a red marker. What now?

That means the last fetch failed. Hover the marker to see why: a site that moved, a feed URL that no longer exists, a temporary outage. Hit ↻ on the feed row to retry right away; if it succeeds, the marker disappears. When the URL has genuinely changed, correct it in Settings → Feeds, where you can also set a custom title, move the feed into a folder, adjust how often it's checked, and store credentials for a feed that sits behind a login.

Where do I see how I'm reading?

Settings → Stats shows what you actually get through: read rate, reading streak, how long you spend on an article, and your most active day and hour. With an AI key it also checks how well relevance scores line up with your real reading, points out missed gems the AI rated highly but you never opened, and estimates what your AI usage costs.

Where do I get an AI key?

Readfine is bring-your-own-key: you create a key with one of the providers and paste it into Settings → AI. It's stored encrypted and used only for your own requests.

  • Anthropic: console.anthropic.com
  • OpenAI: platform.openai.com
  • Google: aistudio.google.com

Running a model yourself needs no key at all, see Can I use my own model?

Can I use my own model or another provider?

Yes. Pick Custom (OpenAI-compatible) as the provider in Settings → AI and fill in the Endpoint field. Anything that speaks the OpenAI API works: Ollama, llama.cpp's server, LM Studio, vLLM, a LiteLLM proxy, or a gateway like OpenRouter. Leave the API key blank for a local server that doesn't ask for one.

  • Ollama's endpoint needs the /v1 suffix (http://localhost:11434/v1); llama.cpp's server does not.
  • One endpoint per account, shared by both slots. Each slot still picks its own model, so a small model can score while a larger one writes summaries, as long as both live on the same server.
  • Self-hosting on a private address (localhost, a Docker network) needs AI_ALLOW_PRIVATE_ENDPOINTS=true in the instance's environment. Without it only public addresses are accepted, which is what keeps a hosted instance from being talked into calling its own internal services.
  • A thinking model is fine, including on both slots. Scoring answers with one number in ten tokens, so a model that reasons first would spend all ten on that; Readfine therefore asks the endpoint to skip reasoning for scoring, which Ollama, llama.cpp and vLLM all understand. Summaries and chat have room to think and are left alone, so one capable model can serve both slots. If your server is too old to know the request, saving the model says so instead of leaving articles unscored.
  • First Verify after a cold start can time out while the server loads the model into memory. Try it once more.
  • Small models have small context windows. If yours is 4k or 8k tokens, lower Article content limit in Settings → AI from its 20 000 character default.
  • Costs can't be shown for your own endpoint: the tokens are counted, the price column stays empty.

What is relevance scoring?

With an AI key, Readfine scores incoming articles against your interest profile: a short description of what you care about, which you write yourself or have the AI generate from the articles you've read, in Settings → AI. Combine scoring with a filter (e.g. mark anything below a threshold as read) to keep your list focused.

How do Catch me up & briefings work?

Catch me up generates an on-demand digest of articles from a period and scope you choose. A briefing is the same thing on a schedule, delivered to your inbox. Both need an AI key. Set them up under Catch me up & briefings in the reader.

How long are articles kept?

Old articles are cleaned up automatically so the archive doesn't grow forever. The exact window is set by whoever runs the instance and is written out at the bottom of Settings → Feeds. Anything you starred, archived or saved by URL is kept indefinitely, so marking an article in any of those ways is how you make sure it stays.

Is there an API?

Yes, a JSON API for scripts and integrations. Create a token in Settings → Tokens and send it as a bearer token.

Is my data private?

Your account, feeds and API keys are yours alone; keys are stored encrypted. See the Privacy Policy for details on what data is held and your rights.
