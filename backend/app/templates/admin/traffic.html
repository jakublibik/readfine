How to read these numbers
  • The daily visitor figures are averages, not a total. Someone who comes back every day is one visitor on each of those days, so adding the days up would count them {{ traffic.days }} times. Page views are totals and do add up.
  • Different people over the window is a range because daily counts cannot pin it down. It cannot be fewer than the best single day, since those people were all here at once, and not more than every day added together, since that counts a regular visitor once per day.
  • A visitor is one IP address and one browser for one day. Two people on a shared connection using the same browser count as one; one person on a phone and a laptop counts as two.
  • Restarting the app inflates that day a little, because anyone who comes back after the restart is counted again. The day's number never goes down.
  • Identified bots is a floor, not the truth. It counts what says so in its user agent, which is most crawler volume; a scraper posing as Chrome is in the human numbers. If this instance is behind Cloudflare, its Security → Bots dashboard is a free second opinion.
  • Sources count human views only, so they total less than page views. direct is almost always the largest: a click from an email, a chat app or a mobile app sends no referrer at all, and most sites send only their bare domain.
  • Only the public pages are counted: landing, sign-in, registration, features, help, privacy and terms. Signed-in visits are skipped, so the numbers for /features and /help are traffic from outside the app.
  • Changing the owner's timezone re-slices past page views correctly, but past visitor days keep the boundaries they were recorded with.
